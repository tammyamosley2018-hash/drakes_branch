Minutes for ApprovalRegular meeting of July 6, 2026 — draft, pending Council action

The regular meeting of the Town Council of Drakes Branch was held Monday, July 6, 2026 at 7:00 p.m. in the Municipal Building located at 4801 Drakes Main Street, Drakes Branch, Virginia.

Mayor Deborah Kennedy called the meeting to order and Stephanie Clay, Clerk, recorded the minutes. Members present were Vice Mayor Peery Wells, Sr., Eugene Wells, Jr., Charles Errickson, James Gregory and Thomas Pettus. Council member Liz Davis was absent. Peery Wells, Sr. gave the invocation and Mayor Deborah Kennedy led the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.

A motion by Charles Errickson to accept the June Regular Council Meeting Minutes and Joint Public Hearing with Planning Commission / Special Council Meeting Minutes was seconded by Peery Wells, Sr., and passed on a unanimous voice vote of all AYES. Absent: Liz Davis.

Account balances & bills

After reviewing all account balances, bills paid and bills to be paid, a motion by Thomas Pettus to accept, seconded by Peery Wells, Sr., passed on a unanimous roll call vote as follows — AYES: Charles Errickson, Eugene Wells, Jr., James Gregory, Thomas Pettus and Peery Wells, Sr. NAYS: None. Absent: Liz Davis.

Speakers

David West addressed the Council and commended Councilman Gregory on the success of the 100-mile yard sale.

Committee reports

Finance
None.
Streets
Peery Wells, Sr. spoke about addressing the grass not being maintained at Dollar General. Clerk Clay stated she would get an ordinance violation notice out to DG immediately.
Museum
None.
Buildings & Grounds
Needs to determine if the Ladies Club is going to continue — if so, a lease is needed.
Water / Sewer
Peery Wells, Sr. addressed the Council and provided an update on the back-up at the pump station. An electrician was coming to look at the power issue the following Tuesday.
Personnel
To be discussed in closed session.
Events
James Gregory gave a summary of the 100-mile yard sale event, which was affected by extreme heat but still had a decent turn out. Mayor Kennedy asked Councilman Gregory to start preparing for a Fall Festival.
Grants
The SLFRF grant program was completed as of Dec. 25, 2025 and has been officially closed out as of June 2026. The Town is still waiting on WACO to provide contract amendments before proceeding with the FEMA project.

Old business

  • Acoustic panels. Tabled until Councilman Gregory can provide more information on purchasing and installing panels by town maintenance. Zach McKinney Technology had provided a bid of $8,235.
  • Chair rails. Eugene Wells received a bid from Chris Cook to install and paint chair rails in the meeting room — $669. Tabled until Councilman Wells could ask Mr. Cook to bid on painting the entire meeting room.

Mayor Kennedy voiced the clerks' request to amend the Town office hours to Monday, Wednesday and Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Thomas Pettus made a motion to accept the new hours, seconded by Charles Errickson. Passed on a roll call vote — AYES: Charles Errickson, James Gregory, Thomas Pettus, Eugene Wells, Jr., Peery Wells, Sr. NAYS: None. Absent: Liz Davis.

AN ORDINANCE TO AMEND THE TOWN CODE OF THE TOWN OF DRAKES BRANCH, VIRGINIA, BY ADDING ARTICLE II, CIGARETTE TAX, TO CHAPTER 12, TAXATION

WHEREAS, Virginia Code § 58.1-3830 authorizes any locality, including the Town of Drakes Branch, to levy a tax upon the sale or use of cigarettes; and

WHEREAS, Virginia Code § 58.1-3832 further authorizes the Town to adopt ordinance provisions for the administration and enforcement of its cigarette tax, including the use of tax stamps, seizure of untaxed cigarettes, and penalties for violations; and

WHEREAS, the Town of Drakes Branch currently has no local cigarette tax in effect; and

WHEREAS, the Town Council finds that the imposition of a local cigarette tax will generate revenue to support town operations and capital needs; and

WHEREAS, notice of a public hearing on this ordinance was published in the Charlotte Gazette, the legal organ of the Town, and a public hearing was held on [HEARING DATE]; and

WHEREAS, adoption of this ordinance imposes a new tax and therefore requires an affirmative vote of two-thirds of the members of the Town Council pursuant to Virginia Code § 15.2-1427(G);

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT ORDAINED by the Town Council of the Town of Drakes Branch, Virginia, that Chapter 12 of the Town Code is amended by adding Article II, Cigarette Tax, as follows:

Article II. Cigarette Tax

Sec. 12-11. Definitions.

The following words, terms and phrases, when used in this article, shall have the meanings ascribed to them in this section, except where the context clearly indicates a different meaning:

Agent means every local dealer and every other person who shall be authorized by the town treasurer to purchase and affix stamps to packages of cigarettes under the provisions of this article.

Dealer means every manufacturer, jobber, wholesale dealer or other person who supplies a seller with cigarettes.

Package means every package, box, can or other container of any cigarettes to which the Internal Revenue Stamp of the United States Government is required to be affixed by and under federal statutes and regulations and in which retail sales of such cigarettes are normally made or intended to be made.

Sale means every act or transaction, irrespective of the method or means employed, including the use of vending machines and other mechanical devices, whereby title to any cigarettes shall be transferred from the seller, as defined in this section, to any other person within the town.

Seller means every person engaged in the business of selling cigarettes who transfers title or in whose place of business title to any such cigarettes is transferred within the town for any purpose other than resale.

CivicLine Clerk — Two-Month PilotCouncil briefing notes

Purpose of this briefing

The Town of Drakes Branch has been selected as the first municipal customer to pilot CivicLine Clerk, a new software platform designed specifically for town clerks and small local governments.

CivicLine is offering the Town a two-month pilot at no cost. During the pilot, Town staff will work directly with CivicLine to set the system up, test it using actual Town procedures, and provide feedback as the platform is developed.

The Mayor attended the meeting with the Town Clerk and supports moving forward. Council is being asked to authorize the Town to participate. The pilot does not commit the Town to continue the service afterward. At the end of the two months, staff will evaluate the results and return to Council with a recommendation before anything further is decided.

Proposed cost after the free pilot

ServiceMonthly cost
CivicLine Clerk administrative platform$450

Regular combined price$690

The Town would pay nothing during the two-month pilot. The $624 monthly fee would only apply if the Town later decides to continue.

What the CivicLine Clerk platform includes

CivicLine Clerk would give Town staff a single organized dashboard to manage routine administrative work, including:

  • FOIA request tracking and deadline alerts
  • Ethics filing reminders
  • Town Council and Planning Commission meeting management
  • Ordinance searches
  • Meeting transcripts and draft minutes
  • Clerk review and certification of minutes
  • Approval workflows and audit trails
  • Connections to Google Drive and other programs the Town already uses

The system can research, organize, draft, track, and route work — but it does not approve or take any official action on its own.

Website AI chatbot

The package also includes an AI chatbot that would be placed on the Town's website. Residents could use it to ask questions about Town ordinances, find forms, services and procedures, get meeting information, begin a FOIA request, and get answers outside of normal Town Hall hours. The chatbot answers questions by searching the Town's own approved records — not just general internet information.

The technology behind it, in plain terms

Council does not need to be technology experts to vote on this proposal, but a brief plain-language explanation of three terms may be helpful.

Microsoft Azure
Microsoft's version of secure cloud storage and computing — the same concept as storing files in Google Drive, but on a larger scale built for business and government applications. CivicLine runs on Azure, which means the Town's data is stored and protected inside Microsoft's secure environment rather than on a local computer that could fail or be lost.
Microsoft Foundry
The management layer that sits on top of Azure. Think of it as the control panel that tells the AI system which Town documents it is allowed to read, who has permission to access what, and how well the system is performing. It keeps the AI focused on approved Town information only and prevents it from going outside the boundaries the Town sets.
RAG — retrieval-augmented generation
Before the AI answers any question, it first searches through the Town's own approved documents to find the most relevant information. It then uses what it finds to form its answer — and the answer can be traced back to the actual Town record it came from. For example, if a resident asks about the grass cutting ordinance, the system looks up the Town's actual ordinance and responds based on that, not a guess. The Town's original records always stay in Google Drive exactly as they are.

Microsoft has stated that customer data used in its AI services is not shared with other customers and is not used to train outside AI systems without permission.

How CivicLine connects to Google Drive

The Town currently has approximately 5,000 to 6,000 files in Google Drive, with more being added as the scanning project continues. CivicLine will connect to the specific Google Drive folders the Town approves — and only those folders. The Town's original files remain in Google Drive and are never moved or altered. Staff will participate in deciding which folders CivicLine is allowed to access during the setup process.

FOIA request management

One of the most practical benefits is how CivicLine would handle FOIA requests from start to finish:

  1. A resident submits a FOIA request through the website.
  2. CivicLine records the request and alerts staff to the legal response deadline.
  3. The system searches approved folders for relevant records.
  4. A draft response or document list is prepared.
  5. The request is routed to the appropriate Town official.
  6. A staff member reviews, approves, or corrects the response.
  7. The approved response goes to the resident.
  8. The system keeps a complete record of every step taken.

The AI does not release any records on its own. A staff member must review and approve every response before anything goes out.

Council and Planning Commission meetings

If a meeting is recorded through Zoom, Google Meet, or another compatible platform, CivicLine can receive the recording transcript and organize the discussion into a readable format, identify motions and votes, prepare a draft set of minutes, route the draft to the Town Clerk for review and corrections, and allow the Clerk to certify the final official minutes. The AI prepares the draft; the Town Clerk remains fully responsible for the official minutes.

Human approval is always required

CivicLine is an administrative assistant — not a decision-maker. Every official action still requires a person to review and approve it, including FOIA responses and release of records, meeting minutes, ordinance-related responses, official correspondence, and administrative approvals. The system routes work to the right person and waits. Nothing moves forward until a staff member acts on it.

Cost and staffing perspective

To put the $624 monthly cost in everyday terms, consider what a part-time Town employee costs over the same period:

CivicLine is not a replacement for the Town Clerk or Deputy Clerk. It is a tool that takes on a significant portion of routine, time-consuming work — and unlike a staff member, it is available around the clock, seven days a week, without overtime or benefits costs.

How the freed staff time would be used

The greatest value of CivicLine is the time it gives back to existing staff. Rather than spending hours searching documents, tracking deadlines, and preparing first drafts, the Town Clerk and Deputy Clerk could focus on:

  • Resolving the backlog of records and administrative issues dating back to 2004–2005
  • Correcting past filings and incomplete documentation
  • Organizing and indexing historical records
  • Updating the Town Code and ordinance files
  • Continuing the scanning and digitization project
  • Improving office procedures and internal controls
  • Preparing stronger grant applications and bringing outside funding into the Town
  • Identifying grants for infrastructure, public facilities, and downtown improvement
  • Addressing blighted and deteriorated properties
  • Supporting economic development and Town marketing

The Town's own grant profile identifies limited administrative capacity and records-management needs as areas that must be strengthened. CivicLine directly addresses both by reducing the routine workload on existing staff.

Benefits of the pilot

The two-month pilot gives the Town a no-risk opportunity to determine whether CivicLine can save meaningful staff time; improve FOIA deadline tracking and compliance; speed up ordinance and records research; improve the quality and timeliness of meeting minutes; provide residents better access to Town information; strengthen accountability through audit trails; help prevent today's work from becoming tomorrow's backlog; and free up staff time for grants, redevelopment, and long-term planning.

Recommended Council action

Recommended action

Authorize the Town of Drakes Branch to participate in the free two-month CivicLine Clerk pilot and permit the Mayor and Town Clerk to execute the written pilot agreement, subject to review of its final terms.

Participation in the pilot does not obligate the Town to purchase the service. At the conclusion of the pilot, staff will evaluate the platform and return to Council with a full recommendation before any paid subscription begins.
